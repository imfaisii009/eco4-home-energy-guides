Great British Insulation Scheme vs ECO4: what changed and what to apply for now

By ApplyFreeBoiler.uk, run by Reliant Energy Solutions Ltd, an ECO4 heating and insulation installer based in Bury, Greater Manchester. We are not the government, Ofgem, your council or an energy supplier.

For a few years, households looking for help with insulation kept running into two names: the Great British Insulation Scheme (GBIS) and ECO4. Both were run through energy suppliers, both were regulated by Ofgem, and both could fund loft and cavity wall insulation. It is no surprise people mixed them up. This guide explains how they differed, what has changed now that GBIS has closed, and where to look for help today.

The short version

  • GBIS has closed. It closed on 31 March 2026, and measures had to be completed before 1 April 2026. You can no longer apply for it.
  • ECO4 is still open until 31 December 2026, for households who meet its rules.
  • In England, the Warm Homes: Local Grant, applied for through GOV.UK and delivered by councils, is another route for eligible lower-income households.

If someone contacts you offering a GBIS installation now, treat it with caution. Ask which scheme is actually funding the work and check the company on Companies House and TrustMark.

How GBIS worked

GBIS was designed to reach a wider group of households than ECO4, but with a narrower offer. In most cases it funded a single insulation measure, such as cavity wall or loft insulation, rather than a package. Eligibility was based mainly on the home's EPC rating and its council tax band, and there was a separate low-income group. Some homes could also get a heating control upgrade alongside the insulation.

Because it was built around one measure per home, GBIS suited households whose main problem was a single gap, for example an empty cavity or a thinly insulated loft.

How ECO4 works

ECO4 takes a whole-house approach. It is aimed at low-income and vulnerable households, usually through a qualifying benefit (the Help to Heat route) or a council referral under LA Flex. A survey decides which combination of measures will improve the home enough to meet the scheme rules. That can include:

  • Loft, cavity wall, solid wall (internal or external), underfloor or room-in-roof insulation.
  • Heating upgrades, such as replacing a broken or inefficient boiler, first-time central heating, or an air source heat pump.
  • Heating controls and ventilation to go with the insulation.

Work follows the PAS 2035 retrofit standard, so the measures are planned together rather than installed in isolation.

Side by side

  • Scope: GBIS usually funded one insulation measure; ECO4 can fund a package across insulation and heating.
  • Who it was for: GBIS reached more households, including many not on benefits, based on EPC and council tax band; ECO4 focuses on low-income and vulnerable households.
  • Timing: GBIS closed on 31 March 2026; ECO4 runs until 31 December 2026.
  • Heating: GBIS did not fund boilers; ECO4 can, where the rules allow.

I started a GBIS application. What now?

If your GBIS work was not completed before 1 April 2026, it cannot go ahead under GBIS. That does not necessarily mean you have missed out. Depending on your household and your home, you may qualify for ECO4, or for the Warm Homes: Local Grant if you live in England. Ask the company that handled your enquiry whether they can assess you for another scheme, or contact your council's energy or housing team.

Which route to try first

  1. If someone in the home receives a qualifying benefit such as Universal Credit or Pension Credit, check ECO4 first, and do it soon because of the December 2026 end date.
  2. If you are not on benefits but have a low household income, ask your council whether you could be referred under LA Flex, and check the Warm Homes: Local Grant on GOV.UK if you are in England.
  3. If you live in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, look at the devolved schemes, such as Home Energy Scotland, Nest in Wales, or the Northern Ireland Housing Executive's programmes.

Spotting confusion and scams

The closure of one scheme and the end date of another create openings for misleading cold calls. Genuine installers will not ask for a fee to apply, will tell you which scheme is funding the work, and will give you their registered company details. Never share bank details on a cold call about a grant.

Common questions

Is there a replacement for GBIS? In England, the government's Warm Homes Plan and the Warm Homes: Local Grant are the main routes for insulation support for eligible households. Check GOV.UK for the current details.

Can I still get cavity wall insulation funded? Possibly, through ECO4 or a council scheme, if your household and home qualify.
