Your first winter with a new condensing boiler: a practical UK guide

By ApplyFreeBoiler.uk, run by Reliant Energy Solutions Ltd, an ECO4 heating and insulation installer based in Bury, Greater Manchester. We are not the government, Ofgem, your council or an energy supplier.

A new boiler should make winter easier, but the first few cold months can still bring questions. Why are the radiators not as hot as before? What is that pressure gauge for? Why did the boiler stop on the coldest morning of the year? This guide covers the practical things worth knowing in your first winter with a new condensing boiler, whether it was fitted under a grant scheme such as ECO4 or paid for privately.

Know what you have

Before the weather turns, find the manufacturer's user guide and the warranty paperwork your installer left. Note down:

  • The boiler make and model, and whether it is a combi, system or regular boiler.
  • The warranty length and any conditions, such as registering it with the manufacturer or having it serviced every year.
  • The installer's contact details and the date of installation.
  • The Gas Safe certificate or building regulations compliance notice for the work.

Many manufacturer warranties require annual servicing by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Missing a service can affect a claim, so put a reminder in your calendar.

Flow temperature: why lower can be better

Condensing boilers are most efficient when the water returning to them is cool enough to condense the water vapour in the flue gases. That recovers heat that would otherwise be lost. If the boiler is set to send very hot water to the radiators, it may not condense much of the time.

Many heating advisers suggest setting the radiator flow temperature to around 60°C or a little lower, then checking whether your home stays comfortable. Radiators will feel warm rather than scorching, and rooms may take slightly longer to heat up. If your home has a hot water cylinder, do not turn the hot water temperature down too far, as stored hot water needs to be kept hot enough to control bacteria. Your user guide or installer can tell you which dial does what.

Use the controls you were given

A new boiler usually comes with a programmer or smart thermostat and thermostatic radiator valves (TRVs). A few habits help:

  • Set the main thermostat to a comfortable temperature and leave it there, rather than turning it up high to warm the house faster. It will not heat up any quicker.
  • Use the timer so the heating comes on shortly before you need it.
  • Turn TRVs down in rooms you use less, such as spare bedrooms, but avoid turning them fully off in cold weather if damp is an issue.
  • Avoid fitting a TRV on the radiator in the same room as the main thermostat, as the two can work against each other.

Boiler pressure

Most sealed heating systems have a pressure gauge on or under the boiler. Your user guide will give the right range, and for many boilers it is roughly 1 to 1.5 bar when the system is cold. The pressure rises a little when the heating is on.

If it keeps dropping, there may be a leak, or air has been bled from radiators. Many boilers have a filling loop to top the pressure up, and the manual explains how. If you are not confident, or the pressure keeps falling, call the installer or a Gas Safe engineer rather than guessing.

Frozen condensate pipes

Condensing boilers produce a little water, which drains through a plastic condensate pipe, often outside. In a hard frost it can freeze, and the boiler may stop and show a fault code. If that happens:

  1. Find where the pipe runs outside and look for the frozen section, usually at a bend or the open end.
  2. Pour warm water, not boiling water, over it, or hold a hot water bottle against it.
  3. Reset the boiler following the manual.

Ask your installer whether the pipe could be insulated or re-routed if it freezes often. Never try to open the boiler casing yourself.

Bleeding radiators

If a radiator is cold at the top and warm at the bottom, there is probably air in it. Bleeding it with a radiator key releases the air. Do it with the heating off and the radiators cool, keep a cloth handy, and check the boiler pressure afterwards, as bleeding can lower it.

Safety basics

  • Fit an audible carbon monoxide alarm near the boiler if you do not already have one, and test it regularly.
  • Do not block vents or the flue terminal outside.
  • If you smell gas, call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999 straight away.

Common questions

Why does my new boiler make a gurgling or clicking noise? Some noise is normal as the system heats and cools, but persistent banging or whistling is worth reporting to the installer while you are still in warranty.

Who services a boiler fitted under ECO4? Any Gas Safe registered engineer can, though check your warranty terms first.
